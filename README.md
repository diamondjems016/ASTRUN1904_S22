This repository contains materials for ASTUN1904, a non-major astronomy lab at Columbia University, that was taught in Spring 2022.
This lab focused on phenomena beyond the Solar System, including but not limited to exoplanets, dark matter, stellar spectroscopy, and Hubble expansion.
The particular section of the lab I taught had 6 students, making it easy to solicit and incorporate student feedback.
The second time I taught the lab in Spring 2023 (materials in public repo ASTRUN1904_S23), reflects some of that feedback.

Syllabus--
The first section is devoted to the class overview, which makes clear the learning objectives of
the class as well as a quick breakdown of the class structure. Doing this makes the class
expectations easy to find for the students.
This is followed by details on the materials that students are expected to bring to class. This
helps to ensure that students are at least materially prepared to participate in the class. We
also are sure to make clear the materials that may be difficult financially for students to obtain
(i.e. laptops) are available to them for the class.
The grading breakdown sets clear expectations for how students should expect to be evaluated
in the class. Of particular note is the participation section. In the past, the participation section
was, for lack of a better description, a fudge factor, for which there were no clear guidelines for
how to do well beyond “ask questions and participate in class”. In this updated policy sheet,
multiple avenues for engagement with the class were provided to ensure that students were
able to participate in a way they were comfortable with.
Finally, we included policies for attendance, accommodations, and academic honesty to make
clear to students what would and would not be tolerated in the class, and what steps they need
to take for exceptions to the policies listed in the document.
